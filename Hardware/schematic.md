## 1. Power tree

```
12 V accessory socket (ignition-switched)
  │
  ├─[ 2 A fuse ]──[ TVS SMBJ26A ]──[ 100 µF / 50 V ]
  │                    (clamps load-dump transients)
  │
  └──► Buck converter, 12 V → 5 V @ 3 A
         │
         ├──► Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W   (5 V / GND header pins 2 and 4 / 6)
         │
         ├──► ESP32-C6-DevKitC-1      (5V pin, or USB)
         │       └── on-board LDO ──► 3V3 rail ──► MPU-6050 VCC
         │
         └──► Motor rail (5 V, or 3 V if using series resistors)
```

**For bench and demo work, skip all of this and use a USB power bank.** The
12 V front end only matters once the system lives in a moving vehicle.

The automotive 12 V rail is hostile: ISO 16750-2 specifies load-dump surges to
35 V on modern centrally-suppressed systems. A bare buck module rated 40 V
absolute max will not survive one. The fuse, TVS and bulk capacitance are not
optional garnish.

## 2. Vibration motor driver

One MOSFET switching the whole array is sufficient — the firmware drives all
motors together.

```
                              +5 V (or +3 V motor rail)
                                 │
                                 ├──────────────┐
                                 │              │
                              [ M1 ]         [ D1 ]      D1 cathode to +V
                            ERM motors      1N4148       ("stripe end up")
                            4× parallel        │
                                 │              │
                                 └──────┬───────┘
                                        │
                                     ┌──┴──┐
                        GPIO 10 ─────┤ G   │  Q1  AO3400A / IRLZ44N
                              100 Ω  │  D  │      logic-level N-MOSFET
                                     │  S  │
                                     └──┬──┘
                                        │
                         10 kΩ ─────────┤        gate pull-down to GND
                                        │
                                       GND  (motor ground — star point)
```

Component values:

| Part | Value | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | AO3400A (SOT-23) or IRLZ44N (TO-220) | V_GS(th) < 2 V so it fully enhances at 3.3 V |
| D1 | 1N4148 (< 200 mA) or 1N4001 | Flyback. **Omitting this destroys the MOSFET and possibly the C6** |
| R_gate | 100 Ω | Limits inrush into gate capacitance |
| R_pd | 10 kΩ | Holds the gate low while the C6 is in reset and GPIO 10 floats |
| C_bulk | 100–470 µF | Absorbs switch-on inrush; place near the motor rail |
| R_series | see §5.2 in README | Only if dropping 5 V to a 3 V motor |

**Diode orientation is the thing to double-check.** Cathode (striped end) to
the positive rail. Backwards, it is a dead short across the supply the moment
power is applied.

## 3. Piezo buzzer — differential drive

```
   GPIO 11 ───[ R6 100 Ω ]───┐ pin 1
                            [ LS1 ]   passive piezo, 5 kHz
   GPIO 18 ───[ R7 100 Ω ]───┘ pin 2
                 (neither leg goes to GND)
```

**Why two pins.** A piezo is a capacitor. The rev 2 low-side MOSFET (Q2, R3,
R4) was quiet because an open-drain switch can charge it through the supply
but cannot actively pull it back. Driving it straight from GPIOs was louder,
and driving it from **two** GPIOs in opposite phase doubles the swing: the
element sees ±3.3 V = **6.6 Vpp**, about +6 dB over one pin against ground. The
two GPIOs act as a tiny H-bridge. **Q2, R3 and R4 are removed.**

**Firmware** (`piezo.cpp`): both pins are attached to the **same LEDC
channel**; GPIO 18's output is inverted in the GPIO matrix, so the two waves
are locked exactly 180° apart. 5000 Hz, 50 % duty (`PIEZO_FREQ_HZ`).

**Off means both pins LOW.** With GPIO 18 inverted, a "duty 0" would leave it
HIGH and hold 3.3 V DC across the element. `piezoOff()` detaches both pins
from LEDC and drives both LOW.

- **R6 / R7 (100 Ω)** limit the edge current. Each edge charges the element
  through 200 Ω: about 33 mA peak, decaying within microseconds. Do not leave
  them out.
- **LS1 must be passive.** An active buzzer has its own oscillator and ignores
  the frequency. No flyback diode: a piezo is capacitive, not inductive.
- **5 kHz** is the assumed resonance. `SWEEP` on the serial console plays
  4–6 kHz in 100 Hz steps; if a different step is clearly louder, change
  `PIEZO_FREQ_HZ` in `config.h`.

## 5. MPU-6050

```
   ESP32-C6              MPU-6050 breakout
   ────────              ─────────────────
   3V3     ──────────────  VCC
   GND     ──────────────  GND
   GPIO 21 ──────────────  SDA
   GPIO 22 ──────────────  SCL
                           AD0 ── GND   (selects I2C address 0x68)
```

- **3.3 V only.** Many breakouts have an onboard regulator and tolerate 5 V on
  VCC, but the C6's I2C lines are 3.3 V — never feed 5 V logic to them.
- Most breakouts include **4.7 kΩ pull-ups** on SDA and SCL. Do not add another
  set; paralleled pull-ups over-load the bus.
- Keep the I2C leads short and away from motor wiring. This is the circuit most
  vulnerable to the switching noise described in README §5.4.
- Bus runs at **100 kHz** and carries only the MPU-6050 (a 3.3 V part: no
  level shifter).

## 5a. Driver screen, voice and button: Whisplay HAT on the Pi Zero (rev 6)

```
 U2 Pi Zero 2 W  ══ 40-pin header ══  U12 PiSugar Whisplay HAT
                                         1.69" 240 x 280 LCD (SPI0) · RGB LED · button
                                         WM8960 audio (I2S + I2C1) · speaker / PH2.0 socket
 U1 ESP32-C6 ── UART status JSON (1 Hz: score, level, links) ──► U2 ──► U12
```
- **No wires:** the HAT plugs onto all 40 pins. Its pinout, screens, LED and
  button are in [`whisplay-hat.md`](whisplay-hat.md).
- **Who drives what:** the ESP32 keeps the buzzer and motors, so the
  alert works without the Pi. The HAT is the driver-facing display and voice.
- **Software:** `Raspberry/drifts/whisplay_ui.py`, inside `python -m drifts`
  when `[whisplay] enabled = true`. Test without the ESP32:
  `python -m drifts.whisplay_ui --demo`.

## 5b. Pi Zero to ESP32: UART, 3 wires (rev 7, firmware v21)

```
   ESP32-C6 (U1)                         Pi Zero 2 W (U2)
   GPIO 19  UART1 TX ──[R10 1k]────────►  GPIO 15 RX  (pin 10)
   GPIO 20  UART1 RX ◄────────[R11 1k]──  GPIO 14 TX  (pin 8)
   GND      ───────────────────────────  GND         (pin 6)
```
- **Both sides are 3.3 V:** no level shifter. R10 / R11 limit the current if
  one board is powered and the other isn't (the input's protection diode would
  otherwise feed it).
- **TX to RX.** The most common mistake is TX to TX: nothing breaks, nothing works.
- **115200 baud, 8N1, one text line per message** (`Esp32/drifts_esp32/pi_uart.h`).
  ESP32 → Pi: `WAKE`, `IDLE`, `STATUS {json}` once a second, `HELLO v21`.
  Pi → ESP32: `PING` every second (the alert fail-safe), `DROWSY`, `NOT_DROWSY`, `EYE:<n>`, `BEEP`.
- **Power:** either one 5 V input through the Pi (§5c, the default), or a
  separate 5 V feed to each board. The Pi's 5V header pins connect straight to
  its USB power input, so a Pi software crash does not cut the ESP32's power:
  only unplugging the supply does.
- **In the car:** twist the three wires together, use a locking connector,
  and keep them away from the motor and piezo leads. At 115200 baud a 1–2 m
  run is fine.
- **On the Pi:** `enable_uart=1`, `dtoverlay=disable-bt` (the Pi's Bluetooth
  isn't needed any more, and this gives GPIO 14/15 the full UART,
  `/dev/serial0` → `ttyAMA0`), and no login console on the serial port.
  `deploy/install.sh` does all three.

## 5c. Power: one 5 V input, through the Pi (default)

One USB supply (5 V, **3 A**) plugs into the Pi's micro-USB **PWR IN**. The
ESP32, the motors, the piezo and the MPU-6050 all take their power from the
Pi's 5V header pin.

```
 USB supply 5 V 3 A ──► Pi Zero 2 W micro-USB (PWR IN)
                         │
 Pi pin 2 or 4 (5V) ─────┴──────────► ESP32 5V pin ──┬──► VCC of the 4 motor modules
                                                     └──► C1 470–1000 µF to GND (at the modules)
 Pi pin 6 (GND) ────────────────────► ESP32 GND ─────────► motor, piezo, MPU-6050 ground
 Pi pin 10 (GPIO 15 RX) ◄──[R10 1k]── ESP32 GPIO 19 (TX)
 Pi pin 8  (GPIO 14 TX) ──[R11 1k]──► ESP32 GPIO 20 (RX)
```

| Pi Zero 2 W | Through | ESP32-C6 | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pin 2 or 4 (5V) | — | 5V | power for the ESP32 and the motors |
| pin 6 (GND) | — | GND | common ground |
| pin 10 (GPIO 15 RX) | R10 1 kΩ | GPIO 19 (TX) | ESP32 → Pi |
| pin 8 (GPIO 14 TX) | R11 1 kΩ | GPIO 20 (RX) | Pi → ESP32 |

- **Current budget:** Pi + camera + Whisplay (~1.3 A at loud audio), ESP32
  (~0.3 A) and 4 motors (0.4–0.6 A, more at start) peak near **2 A**, all through
  the Pi's micro-USB (rated ~2.5 A). Use a **5 V 3 A** supply and a short, thick
  cable. C1 at the motors stops their start-up current from dipping the Pi
  into undervoltage.
- **The IR board is not on this budget.** Its 3 W LED (0.6–1 A) is fed from the
  5 V supply ahead of the Pi, not from the Pi's pins: [camera-ir.md](camera-ir.md).
- **Motor VCC on 5V, not 3V3:** the ESP32 board's 3.3 V regulator (~500–600 mA,
  shared with the ESP32's own Bluetooth peaks) can't also start four motors. If
  the motors are 3 V parts, lower `DROWSY_VIB_DUTY` in `config.h` (~150).
- **Never two 5 V sources at once:** unplug the 5V wire from the ESP32 before
  plugging its USB-C into a laptop to flash or use the serial monitor (keep GND
  and the two data wires). Many dev boards don't stop two supplies feeding
  each other.
- **Pins 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 under the Whisplay HAT:** use a stacking header, or
  solder the wires to the pads on the underside of the Pi.
- **In the car:** the USB supply is a car charger in the lighter socket (it has
  its own 12 V → 5 V converter, so U6 is not needed). Wiring straight to 12 V
  instead needs U6, F1 and D2 (§1). For more margin, a 2-port charger can feed
  the ESP32 separately (then leave Pi pin 2/4 unconnected to the ESP32; keep GND).

## 7. Bring-up order

Do not power the whole system at once. Each step isolates a failure the next
would hide.

1. **C6 alone**, USB powered. Confirm it boots and the serial console prints at
   115200.
2. **Add the MPU-6050.** Confirm `[IMU] MPU-6050 at 0x68` on boot. If absent,
   the bus is miswired or the address is wrong.
3. **Add the buzzer** (R6, R7, LS1 between GPIO 11 and 18). `TEST:0:2000`
   should produce a 5 kHz tone. Verify before the motors, because it is the
   quieter failure.
4. **Add the motor branch with a bench supply and a current meter in series.**
   Confirm the current matches expectation before connecting it to the shared
   rail. This is where a missing flyback diode reveals itself cheaply.
5. **Add the Pi**, on its own supply first. Then combine rails and confirm the
   5 V does not sag below 4.75 V under simultaneous load.
6. **Then** the dongle and the full BLE bring-up.

Measure the 5 V rail with a scope at step 5 if one is available. A rail that
dips on motor switch-on is what reboots the Pi mid-demo.
